# Where the interface stands against the GUI specification

[GUI_SPEC.md](GUI_SPEC.md) arrived on 2026-10-12, as Phase 1 was being finished. This page lists,
section by section, what LCAD has of it and what is missing, with the phase each gap is
scheduled for. It is updated at the end of every phase.

Status as of Phase 5 (2026-10-19), components and assemblies, in progress. Phase 1b, the
interface shell phase that this specification added to the [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md), was
finished on 2026-10-12.

| Section | Have | Missing, and when |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Originality | LCAD's own application icon, document icon, and tool icons (line drawings in one style, drawn in the theme's colors). Layout, colors, and controls are LCAD's own, built from general CAD conventions. | The orientation cube is OCCT's stock one, in the theme's colors, with its sides in three shades and its edges and corners dark so that the sides can be told apart. An orientation control of LCAD's own design: Phase 3, with the other in-view controls. |
| 2. Visual direction | Design tokens (`Theme.h`) for every surface, text, state, and viewport color, and for radius, spacing, toolbar height, and icon size, in a light and a dark set. The palette, the style sheet, the icons, and the 3D view all take their values from them. One accent color. | Type sizes follow the system's font, with one smaller size for group labels; a fuller scale when dense panels need it. The labels in the 3D view use the fonts OCCT finds, not the interface's. |
| 3. Main window | Header, toolbar, browser left, viewport center, properties right, history strip bottom, status bar. Panels resize, hide, float, and re-dock. The layout is remembered between launches, and Window > Reset Layout restores the default. The viewport has more than half the window at 1280 by 800. | |
| 4. Header | Menus; new, open, save, undo, redo with tooltips; document tabs with an unsaved mark; command search; warning before closing unsaved work; Ctrl+N, O, S, Z, Shift+Z. No sign-in exists. | A workspace selector, if a later phase brings a workspace (see 5). Update notification: when there are releases to be notified of. |
| 5. Workspaces | One design workflow; nothing needs a mode switch. Assemblies are not a workspace of their own: their tools are an **Assemble** group in the toolbar and an Assemble menu, beside the modeling tools, as the specification's advice on contextual visibility suggests. | Drawing and manufacture arrive with Phase 6, and whether they need workspaces is decided then. |
| 6. Toolbar | Create, Modify, Assemble, Select, and View groups, with icons and labels; every tool has a tooltip and is found by the command search. While a sketch is open the toolbar is the sketch's: Draw, Modify, and Constrain groups, and Finish Sketch on the right. In a narrow window the primitives give up their labels, then New Component and Joint fold into the assembly button beside them (which lists them too), and the twelve constraints of the sketch toolbar fold into one button that lists them, before anything is pushed out of sight. | Construct and Inspect have no groups of their own: their tools are in the menus and the View group. |
| 7. Browser | Document root, origin planes and axes, bodies, sketches; expand, select, multiselect, rename inline, show and hide, isolate, show all, filter by name, context menu, delete. A double-click opens a sketch, and the sketch that is open is marked in bold. Actions that do not apply are not offered. Features, with suppression and failure marks, are in the timeline. Components: each occurrence with its own bodies, sketches, construction geometry, and joints, nested; double-click to work on one (shown in bold); drag one onto another to move it there; joints listed with their state, ticked to suppress. | |
| 8. Viewport | Orbit, pan, zoom, fit, zoom to selection, standard and isometric views, both projections, orientation cube that can be clicked or dragged; two navigation presets and a reversible wheel; selection of bodies, faces, edges, vertices with hover outline and selection tint; selection filters; rectangle selection; choosing among overlapping objects (Select Other); reference grid, axis marker, origin guides, each switchable; display styles: shaded with edges, shaded, wireframe, hidden line, transparent; light and dark colors. The view cube carries red, green, and blue X, Y, Z arrows; the planes a new sketch is chosen on are slightly see-through. Each occurrence of a component is drawn where it is; joints in progress show their frames as small X, Y, Z arrows, and overlaps found by Check Interference are drawn in red. A pull has an arrow that is dragged to set its distance. | Controls mounted on the viewport's edges: display style, grid, and fit are in the toolbar and the View menu instead. Phase 3, with section analysis. Silhouette edges drawn differently from inner edges. |
| 9. Properties panel | A right-side panel that holds the command in progress (live preview, validated numbers, errors in plain words, Enter confirms, Esc cancels) and otherwise the selection: a body's name and visibility, editable, and its volume, area, size, center of mass, and counts; a face's area, an edge's length, a vertex's position. Its width does not change with its content. A selected sketch shows its state, and how many curves, constraints, and closed outlines it has. While a sketch is open the panel is about the sketch: whether it is fully constrained, the options of the drawing tools, what is selected, and the constraints on it, each with a button that removes it. A body's material is chosen here, and its mass shown; an occurrence shows its component, where it is, whether it is grounded or linked, and what holds it; a joint shows its kind, its parts, its values, and whether it holds. | |
| 10. Timeline | Every sketch, feature, and body put in as it is, in order, with icons and a tooltip that says what it is, what it depends on, and why it fails. Select, edit (double-click or Enter; the model rolls back to just before it), rename, suppress and restore, delete, reorder by dragging (refused with the reason when that would put a feature before what it depends on), the history marker dragged to look at or work on an earlier state, failure markers, horizontal scrolling, a compact size. Distinct looks for selected, suppressed, failing, being edited, and past the marker. | Collapsing groups of entries (there are no groups yet); warnings that are not failures (no feature produces any yet). |
| 11. Command search | S or Ctrl+K. Partial names, aliases, groups, keyboard navigation, Enter, Esc, recent commands. Commands that cannot be started now are listed last and cannot be run. It starts the real commands. | Ranking by the kind of thing selected, when there are enough commands for it to matter (Phase 3). |
| 12. Context menus | Browser: rename, hide or show, isolate, delete. Viewport: Select Other, zoom to selection, hide, isolate, delete, show all, fit, home. Only what applies is listed. On a flat face: New Sketch. On a sketch: Edit Sketch. Inside an open sketch the menu is about the sketch: dimension, construction, the constraints, delete, and Finish Sketch. | Modify a face: with that command (Phase 4). A marking menu is not planned for now. |
| 13. Sketch interface | A new sketch's plane is chosen in the 3D view: the three origin planes are shown, the one under the pointer lights up, and a click takes it; a flat face of a body does as well. Opening a sketch turns the camera to face its plane, moves the grid into it, swaps the toolbar for the sketch tools, and says so in the status bar; Finish Sketch (a toolbar button, a panel button, and Ctrl+Enter) puts the camera and the tools back. Tools: line, rectangle, circle, arc, ellipse, spline, point, polygon, slot, offset, trim, extend, mirror, project, dimension, and twelve constraints. Geometry is drawn in one color while it can still move and another when it is fully constrained; construction geometry is dashed; projected references have a color of their own; a closed outline is tinted. Dimensions are drawn next to what they measure and are opened by a double-click. A line is dimensioned with one click and its length typed straight away, the dimension following the pointer until it is placed; D on a selected line opens its length. The side panel says what the tool in use waits for. Nothing but the toolbar and the side panel covers the sketch. | Sketch pattern. Dimension values cannot be dragged to another place yet. |
| 14. In-canvas numbers | A dimension's value is typed in a field that opens in the 3D view at the dimension. While a line, a rectangle, or a circle is being drawn, typing a number gives its length, its width and height, or its diameter. The field shows what is being edited, takes a unit after the number, and when the text is not a number or the geometry cannot take the value, keeps the text, marks the field, and says what is wrong. A pull's distance is typed into a box beside its arrow in the 3D view, as a number with a unit or an expression of parameters, negative for the other way. | A line's angle while drawing. |
| 15. Tabs | Several documents in tabs: switch, new, close, unsaved mark, each with its own camera, undo history, and origin guides. A file is never opened twice. Recent files, with their folders. | Thumbnails in the recent list (the files already carry them). |
| 16. File dialogs | New, open, save, save as, with the `.lcad` filter; the last folder is remembered. Import (STEP, IGES) is separate from Open, and says what was done to the geometry; Export asks for the format first and shows only that format's settings, says that a mesh is an approximation, and remembers its folder and settings. Files go where the user says and nowhere else. Insert Part opens another LCAD document as a linked part; Package Project asks for the folder to gather the project into. Copies of unsaved work are kept in LCAD's own state folder, never beside the user's files. | |
| 17. Notifications | Status bar: what is selected and its measurement, the command in progress, the units, and brief messages for saved and opened. Modal dialogs only for unsaved changes, for errors that stop an action, and for restoring work after a crash. Opening a document says when a linked part changed or cannot be found. | A progress display, when there is an operation long enough to need one (Phase 3). |
| 18. Themes | Light and dark, from central tokens, checked for contrast by a test. The choice is remembered. "Follow the System" is the default. | |
| 19. Layout | Resize, hide, re-dock, remember, reset; hide the timeline; Focus on 3D View (F11). | Compact and comfortable density modes: with the first dense panel, in Phase 3. |
| 20. Mouse and keyboard | Standard shortcuts, Esc, Enter, Delete, F2, undo and redo, tab order through panel fields. Window > Keyboard Shortcuts changes any shortcut and refuses one already in use. | |
| 21. Window sizes | Tested at 1024 by 700, 1280 by 800, and 1600 by 1000, for modeling and for sketching: nothing overlaps, nothing is clipped, no tool is pushed behind an overflow arrow. Toolbar groups fold as the window narrows. | Below about 1000 pixels wide the toolbars have not been tried. |
| 22. Accessibility | Qt's standard widgets throughout, so keyboard access to menus, focus indicators, and screen-reader roles come with them; tooltips on icon-only buttons; unsaved work, visibility, and command state are shown by marks and check boxes, not by color alone; View > Animate View Changes turns the one animation off. Drawn correctly at twice the display scale. | A scale setting of LCAD's own; today it follows the desktop's. |
| 23. Animation | The view cube's camera turn, which can be switched off, and a joint shown in motion when asked for (Animate). Nothing waits for an animation; Esc stops a joint's. | |
| 24. Architecture | A command registry: each command is defined once, with its name, aliases, shortcuts, icon, and the conditions under which it applies, and the toolbar, menus, context menus, shortcuts, and search all use that definition. The shell, tabs, browser, viewport, properties, search, themes, and icons are separate classes that reach the model only through the document's interface. | Commands with their own parameters and validation as registry objects (today that lives in the command's panel, and for sketch tools in the sketch editor): Phase 3, when features need a common shape for it. |
| 25. Working prototype | All sixteen steps. 1 to 7 and 14 to 16 as before; 8 to 13 in `ModelingInterfaceTest`: Pull with an exact distance, a fillet, the original sketch edited from the browser, the model following, a feature selected and edited from the timeline. | Nothing in the steps themselves. |
| 26. GUI testing | Automated interface tests for all of the above that exists, run at the screen's scale and again at twice that, in both themes and at three window sizes: overlap and clipping, keyboard handling, context menus, selection, tabs, unsaved marks, undo and redo, state after cancel, and what reaches the screen. | A document with many features and many components, to time the interface by. |

## What the specification changes in the plan

1. **An interface shell phase before the sketcher (Phase 1b), now done.** The specification says
   to build the shell and design system first and connect commands to it as they become real.
   The sketcher adds some twenty commands; they will land in a command registry with search,
   themes, and icons already in place.
2. **The name.** The specification says ForgeCAD throughout. The project is LCAD, and nothing in
   the repository uses the other name.
3. **Originality.** The brief says "Fusion-like"; the specification adds that LCAD must not look
   like a copy. What carries over from Fusion is the workflow and the mouse mapping, which are
   conventions, not artwork. Icons, colors, and the orientation control are to be LCAD's own.
